Woodworker's Epoxy Resin FAQ

How do you seal live edge wood before an epoxy river pour?
Always apply a thin seal coat of 1:1 tabletop epoxy onto the raw live edges with a foam brush 4 to 6 hours before your main pour. Wood pores contain trapped air; without a seal coat, the warm curing epoxy pulls air out of the timber, creating persistent bubbles along the wood-epoxy boundary line.
What causes epoxy resin to flash cure or overheat?
Epoxy cures through an exothermic (heat-generating) chemical reaction. If a fast-curing 1:1 resin is poured deeper than its recommended limit (usually 1/4" to 1/2"), the heat cannot dissipate, causing the core temperature to spike above 200°F (93°C). This results in yellowing, thermal stress fractures, and smoke. For depths over 1/2", always use a slow-curing 2:1 deep pour casting resin.
How much pigment or mica powder should I add to epoxy?
A general rule of thumb is 1 to 2 grams of mica powder per 16 fluid ounces of mixed epoxy for a vibrant metallic shimmer. For liquid alcohol inks or translucent dye tints, add 2 to 5 drops at a time until reaching desired opacity. Never exceed 5% total additive by volume, or the curing strength will be compromised.
